> [!definition] Dividend Yield
> Dividend yield is the yearly dividend per share divided by the current share price.

## Explain

A bank deposit pays interest. A dividend is the cash a company shares with owners out of profit. Yield tells you the equivalent 'interest rate' on the price you pay today.

Companies that reinvest most of their profit (like fast-growing ones) pay a small yield. The rest of your return comes from the share price rising, if it does.

> [!formula] Dividend yield
> $$\text{Dividend yield} = \frac{\text{Dividend per share}}{\text{Share price}} \times 100$$
> - Dividend per share: total yearly dividend divided by number of shares

## Data: Reliance

> [!in-practice] Reliance Industries in numbers
> - Dividend yield 0.51% (Screener)
> - Dividend payout 10% of profit in FY26

## Example

> [!example] Reliance dividend per share
> Payout 10% × EPS ₹59.69 = about ₹5.97 per share.
>
> 5.97 ÷ 1,168 = 0.51%. On ₹1,00,000 of Reliance shares that is about ₹511 a year, far less than a bank FD would pay, so investors here rely on price growth.

> [!warning] A high yield can be a warning
> If the price has crashed, the yield jumps. Check if the dividend is paid from profit that will continue.
