> [!definition] Interest Coverage
> Interest expense is what a company pays on its borrowings. Interest coverage is operating profit divided by interest, showing how many times the profit can pay the interest.

## Explain

A lender wants to know: if profit falls, can this company still pay me? A company that earns 6 times its interest is much safer than one earning 1.2 times.

Interest is a fixed cost. In a bad year, sales and profit fall but the interest bill remains.

> [!formula] Interest coverage
> $$\text{Interest coverage} = \frac{\text{Operating profit}}{\text{Interest expense}}$$
> - Operating profit: see [[operating-profit|Operating Profit]]
> - Interest expense: yearly interest paid on [[borrowings|Borrowings]]

## Data: Reliance

> [!in-practice] Reliance Industries in numbers
> - FY26 interest ₹27,061 crore
> - FY26 operating profit ₹1,79,065 crore
> - Borrowings ₹4,02,962 crore (March 2026)

*Source: [Screener.in](https://www.screener.in/company/RELIANCE/consolidated/), Reliance Industries, consolidated, viewed 3 Oct 2026. Amounts in ₹ crore unless stated. Used to show how to read the numbers; this is not investment advice.*

## Example

> [!example] Reliance coverage
> 1,79,065 ÷ 27,061 = 6.6 times.
>
> Even if operating profit fell by half, it would still cover interest 3.3 times.
>
> Rough average cost of borrowing: 27,061 ÷ 4,02,962 = 6.7% a year (this is only an approximation because debt changes during the year).

> [!tip] Lender's rule of thumb
> Many lenders get uneasy below about 2 to 3 times. The right level depends on how steady the business is.

## My Take

> [!my-take] My Take
> _Would you lend to a company whose profit swings a lot, even if its coverage is 6 times today?_
