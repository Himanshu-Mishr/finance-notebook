> [!definition] Balance Sheet
> The balance sheet lists a company's assets (what it owns) and liabilities plus owners' equity (where the money to buy them came from) on a single date. The two sides always match.

## Explain

Imagine buying a flat: part of the money is your savings (equity) and part is a home loan (borrowings). The flat is the asset. A balance sheet does this for a whole company.

Unlike the [[profit-and-loss|Profit and Loss Statement]], which covers a period, the balance sheet is a photograph on one day, usually 31 March.

> [!formula] The balance
> $$\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Owners' equity}$$
> - Owners' equity = [[equity-capital|Equity Capital]] + [[reserves|Reserves]]

## Data: Reliance

> [!in-practice] Reliance Industries in numbers
> - Total assets = total liabilities = ₹21,77,546 crore (March 2026)
> - Equity capital ₹13,532 + reserves ₹8,90,498 + borrowings ₹4,02,962 + other liabilities ₹8,70,554 crore
> - Fixed assets ₹11,24,795 + CWIP ₹2,37,686 + investments ₹2,48,332 + other assets ₹5,66,733 crore

*Source: [Screener.in](https://www.screener.in/company/RELIANCE/consolidated/), Reliance Industries, consolidated, viewed 3 Oct 2026. Amounts in ₹ crore unless stated. Used to show how to read the numbers; this is not investment advice.*

## Example

> [!example] Both sides add up
> Liabilities: 13,532 + 8,90,498 + 4,02,962 + 8,70,554 = 21,77,546.
>
> Assets: 11,24,795 + 2,37,686 + 2,48,332 + 5,66,733 = 21,77,546 ✓.
>
> About 41% of the funding is the owners' own (904,030 ÷ 2,177,546); borrowings are about 19%; the rest is other liabilities such as money owed to suppliers.
