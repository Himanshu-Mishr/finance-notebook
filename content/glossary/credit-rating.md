> [!definition] Credit Rating
> A credit rating is an opinion from a rating agency on how likely a borrower is to repay its debt on time. In India the main agencies are CRISIL, ICRA, CARE and India Ratings.

## Explain

Like a school report card for borrowers. AAA is the highest safety. Lower ratings (AA, A, BBB and down) carry more risk and must pay higher interest to attract lenders.

Short-term debt such as commercial paper has its own scale: A1+ is the highest.

## Data: Reliance

> [!in-practice] Reliance Industries in numbers
> - On 25 Sep 2026 CARE and CRISIL gave AAA/Stable ratings to Reliance's ₹67,000 crore debentures
> - CARE reaffirmed A1+ on ₹34,500 crore of commercial paper

## Example

> [!example] Why AAA matters
> With AAA, Reliance can borrow at lower interest than a weaker company. A difference of 1% on ₹13,008.6 crore (the NCD above) is about ₹130 crore a year.
>
> 'Stable' means the agency does not expect the rating to change soon.

> [!warning] Ratings are opinions
> Agencies have been late to spot trouble before. Use the rating as one input, not the whole answer.
