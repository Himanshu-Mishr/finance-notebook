> [!definition] Operating Profit Margin
> Operating profit margin (OPM) is operating profit divided by sales, shown as a percentage.

## Explain

It tells you how much of every ₹100 of sales is left after paying the costs of running the business. Higher is better, but compare only within the same industry: a supermarket might earn 5% while a software firm earns 30%.

A margin that keeps falling while sales rise is an early warning that costs or competition are biting.

> [!formula] OPM
> $$\text{OPM} = \frac{\text{Operating profit}}{\text{Sales}} \times 100$$
> - Operating profit: see [[operating-profit|Operating Profit]]
> - Sales: see [[sales-revenue|Sales (Revenue)]]

## Data: Reliance

> [!in-practice] Reliance Industries in numbers
> - FY15 10%, FY24 18%, FY26 17%
> - June 2026 quarter 15%, against 18% a year earlier

## Example

> [!example] Reliance margins
> FY26: 1,79,065 ÷ 10,55,780 = 17%.
>
> June 2026: 47,517 ÷ 3,09,468 = 15%; June 2025: 42,905 ÷ 2,43,632 = 18%.
>
> Sales grew but the margin slipped three points. A reader would ask whether that is the lower-margin refining business growing faster than the rest.

> [!critical] Only compare like with like
> Margins differ hugely by industry. A 15% margin may be excellent in one business and poor in another.
