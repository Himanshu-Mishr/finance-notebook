> [!definition] Free Cash Flow
> Free cash flow (FCF) is cash from operations minus capital expenditure. It is the cash that could go to owners and lenders without hurting the business.

## Explain

Operating cash flow is not all free. A refinery or telecom firm must keep spending on plants. What remains after that spending is 'free' cash.

Valuation models (see DCF later in this notebook) often value a company by the free cash it will produce.

> [!formula] FCF
> $$\text{FCF} = \text{Cash from operations} - \text{Capex}$$
> - Capex: spending on fixed assets

## Data: Reliance

> [!in-practice] Reliance Industries in numbers
> - FY26 ₹70,023 crore; FY25 ₹41,079 crore
> - Negative in many years: −₹76,560 crore in FY21, −₹50,431 crore in FY19

## Example

> [!example] From burning to generating
> Reliance had negative free cash flow in 7 of the 12 years shown, while it built Jio, retail and new energy.
>
> FY26 is the strongest year: +70,023 crore. It means the business paid for its own growth and still had cash left.

> [!warning] Definitions differ
> Sites may subtract different capex items. Use the same source across years.
