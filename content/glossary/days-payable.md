> [!definition] Days Payable
> Days payable is the average number of days a company takes to pay its suppliers: payables divided by cost of purchases per day.

## Explain

When a supplier lets you pay in 60 days, they are lending you money for those 60 days at no interest. A strong company can negotiate long payment terms.

Too long can strain suppliers or hint at cash trouble. Falling days payable can mean suppliers want faster payment.

> [!formula] Days payable
> $$\text{Days payable} = \frac{\text{Payables}}{\text{Purchases}} \times 365$$
> - Payables: money owed to suppliers

## Data: Reliance

> [!in-practice] Reliance Industries in numbers
> - FY26: 84 days; FY25: 108; FY18: 146 (the peak)
> - FY15: 74 days

*Source: [Screener.in](https://www.screener.in/company/RELIANCE/consolidated/), Reliance Industries, consolidated, viewed 3 Oct 2026. Amounts in ₹ crore unless stated. Used to show how to read the numbers; this is not investment advice.*

## Example

> [!example] A shrinking cushion
> Days payable fell from 108 to 84 in a year, 24 days. Reliance paid its suppliers faster, which uses up cash.
>
> This is one reason the cash conversion cycle turned from −8 to +25 in FY26 (see [[cash-conversion-cycle|Cash Conversion Cycle]]).
