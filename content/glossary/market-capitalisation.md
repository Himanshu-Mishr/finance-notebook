> [!definition] Market Capitalisation
> Market capitalisation (market cap) is the share price multiplied by the number of shares in issue. It is what the market says the whole company is worth today, not what the company owns or earns.

## Explain

Imagine a company with 100 shares, each trading at ₹50. Anyone wanting to buy the entire company at today's price would need ₹5,000. That is its market cap.

Market cap tells you the size of a business, not whether it is cheap or expensive. A huge company can be overpriced and a tiny one a bargain. SEBI sorts listed companies by size: the top 100 are large-cap, the next 150 mid-cap and the rest small-cap.

> [!formula] Market cap
> $$\text{Market cap} = \text{Share price} \times \text{Number of shares}$$
> - Share price: today's price of one share
> - Number of shares: all shares issued, in the company's books

## Data: Reliance

> [!in-practice] Reliance Industries in numbers
> - Price ₹1,168 (close of 1 Oct 2026)
> - Market cap ₹15,80,194 crore, about ₹15.8 lakh crore
> - Shares: equity capital ₹13,532 crore ÷ face value ₹10 = about 1,353 crore shares
> - One of the largest listed companies in India, firmly large-cap

*Source: [Screener.in](https://www.screener.in/company/RELIANCE/consolidated/), Reliance Industries, consolidated, viewed 3 Oct 2026. Amounts in ₹ crore unless stated. Used to show how to read the numbers; this is not investment advice.*

## Example

> [!example] Rebuilding Reliance's market cap
> 1,353.2 crore shares × ₹1,168 = ₹15,80,538 crore.
>
> Screener shows ₹15,80,194 crore. The small gap is because the price used is the live ₹1,167.7 and not the rounded ₹1,168.

> [!warning] Market cap is not price
> A ₹100 share is not 'cheaper' than a ₹5,000 share. Only price relative to earnings or assets (see [[pe-ratio|P/E Ratio]], [[price-to-book|Price to Book Ratio]]) tells you that.
