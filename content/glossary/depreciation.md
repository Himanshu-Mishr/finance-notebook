> [!definition] Depreciation
> Depreciation spreads the cost of a long-life asset over the years it is used. It is a non-cash expense: the money was spent when the asset was bought.

## Explain

A refinery costs thousands of crores and lasts decades. Showing the whole cost in the year of purchase would make that year look terrible and later years too good. Instead, the cost is spread over the asset's useful life.

Depreciation reduces profit but does not take cash out of the company that year. That is why [[cash-from-operations|Cash from Operations]] is usually higher than net profit for asset-heavy firms.

> [!formula] Straight-line depreciation
> $$\text{Depreciation per year} = \frac{\text{Cost} - \text{Scrap value}}{\text{Useful life in years}}$$
> - Cost: price paid for the asset
> - Scrap value: what it is worth at the end
> - Useful life: years the asset is expected to be used

## Data: Reliance

> [!in-practice] Reliance Industries in numbers
> - FY26 ₹57,688 crore, about 5.5% of sales
> - FY15 ₹11,547 crore, so it grew about five times in 11 years
> - Fixed assets rose from ₹1,56,458 crore to ₹11,24,795 crore over the same period

## Example

> [!example] A simple machine
> A machine costs ₹100 crore, has no scrap value and lasts 10 years.
>
> Depreciation is ₹10 crore a year. Profit is reduced by ₹10 crore each year, but cash left the company only once, at the start.
>
> Reliance's depreciation grew along with its assets: each new plant or telecom network adds a yearly charge.
