> [!definition] Operating Profit
> Operating profit is sales minus the day-to-day costs of running the business, such as raw materials, wages and rent. It is measured before interest, depreciation and tax.

## Explain

It answers: is the business itself making money, ignoring how it is financed and taxed? Two companies with identical operations but different debt will show the same operating profit.

Screener's operating profit is very close to what analysts call EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation), except that it leaves out [[other-income|Other Income]].

> [!formula] Operating profit
> $$\text{Operating profit} = \text{Sales} - \text{Operating expenses}$$
> - Operating expenses: raw materials, employee cost, selling and other costs, but not interest, depreciation or tax

## Example

> [!example] Cost of doing business
> For every ₹100 of sales Reliance spent about ₹83 on operating costs and kept about ₹17 as operating profit (see [[operating-profit-margin|Operating Profit Margin]]).
>
> Compare June 2026 with June 2025: sales up 27% but operating profit up only 11% (47,517 vs 42,905 crore). Costs grew faster than sales.
