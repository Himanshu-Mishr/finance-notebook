> [!definition] Equity Capital
> Equity capital is the number of shares issued times their face value. It is the original, nominal part of the owners' money.

## Explain

It is usually a small number compared with [[reserves|Reserves]], because share prices are many times face value and profits have built up over the years.

If equity capital suddenly doubles with no money coming in, the company probably issued bonus shares: new shares given free to existing shareholders.

## Data: Reliance

> [!in-practice] Reliance Industries in numbers
> - ₹13,532 crore (March 2026)
> - ₹6,766 crore in March 2024, then ₹13,532 crore in March 2025
> - Face value ₹10

*Source: [Screener.in](https://www.screener.in/company/RELIANCE/consolidated/), Reliance Industries, consolidated, viewed 3 Oct 2026. Amounts in ₹ crore unless stated. Used to show how to read the numbers; this is not investment advice.*

## Example

> [!example] Reading the doubling
> In March 2025 equity capital doubled from 6,766 to 13,532 crore while reserves kept growing as usual. That is the footprint of a bonus issue (one extra share for every share held, consistent with the 1-for-1 bonus issue Reliance announced in 2024), not of new money coming in.
>
> The number of shares doubled, so EPS and book value per share halved. That is why data providers restate old per-share figures.

> [!note] Why it matters
> If you compare EPS across years, make sure the share count has been adjusted for bonus issues and splits.
