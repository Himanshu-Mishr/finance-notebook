> [!definition] Face Value
> Face value (or par value) is the nominal value of one share in the company's books. It is an accounting unit and has no link to the market price.

## Explain

When a company is set up it divides its capital into shares with a fixed face value, such as ₹1, ₹2 or ₹10. Equity capital on the balance sheet equals face value times the number of shares.

This gives a useful trick: if you know equity capital and face value, you can work out the number of shares.

> [!formula] Number of shares
> $$\text{Number of shares} = \frac{\text{Equity capital}}{\text{Face value}}$$
> - Equity capital: see [[equity-capital|Equity Capital]]
> - Face value: nominal value per share

## Data: Reliance

> [!in-practice] Reliance Industries in numbers
> - Face value ₹10

## Example

> [!example] How many Reliance shares?
> 13,532 crore ÷ ₹10 = 1,353.2 crore shares, about 135 billion shares.
>
> Multiply by the share price to get [[market-capitalisation|Market Capitalisation]].

> [!note] Face value can change
> Companies split shares (₹10 to ₹5, for example) or issue bonus shares. Prices and EPS in old charts are then restated.
