> [!definition] NCD (Non-Convertible Debenture)
> A non-convertible debenture (NCD) is a bond issued by a company. Investors lend money for a fixed period and receive a fixed interest (the coupon). It cannot be converted into shares.

## Explain

Instead of borrowing from one bank, a company can borrow from many investors by selling NCDs. The company promises the coupon each year and repayment of the principal on the maturity date.

The coupon depends on the issuer's safety (see [[credit-rating|Credit Rating]]) and on market interest rates.

> [!formula] Yearly interest
> $$\text{Interest} = \text{Face amount} \times \text{Coupon rate}$$
> - Face amount: money borrowed
> - Coupon rate: yearly interest rate

## Data: Reliance

> [!in-practice] Reliance Industries in numbers
> - On 1 Oct 2026 Reliance allotted ₹13,008.6 crore of unsecured NCDs at a 7.90% coupon, maturing 1 Oct 2036 (from the company's announcements, as shown on Screener)

## Example

> [!example] What this NCD costs
> 13,008.6 crore × 7.90% = about ₹1,028 crore of interest a year, for 10 years.
>
> Total over the term: about ₹10,280 crore of interest, plus ₹13,008.6 crore of principal repaid in 2036.
>
> That is a small part of Reliance's ₹27,061 crore yearly interest bill (see [[interest-expense-and-coverage|Interest Coverage]]).
