> [!definition] Price to Book Ratio
> The price-to-book (P/B) ratio is the share price divided by book value per share.

## Explain

If P/B is 1, the market values the company at exactly the net worth in its books. Above 1, investors pay a premium, usually because they expect the company to earn good returns on that net worth (see [[roe|Return on Equity]]). Below 1, the market values it at less than its books.

P/B and ROE move together: a company that earns high returns on its equity usually trades at a higher P/B.

> [!formula] P/B
> $$\text{P/B} = \frac{\text{Share price}}{\text{Book value per share}}$$
> - Share price: market price
> - Book value per share: see [[book-value|Book Value]]

## Data: Reliance

> [!in-practice] Reliance Industries in numbers
> - Price ₹1,168; book value ₹668
> - ROE 8.91% (Screener)

## Example

> [!example] Reliance P/B
> 1,168 ÷ 668 = 1.75.
>
> The market pays ₹1.75 for every ₹1 of net worth even though ROE is under 9%. Investors are paying for growth in Jio, retail and new energy, not for current returns.

> [!warning] Low P/B can be a trap
> A company trading below book value may be cheap, or may have assets worth less than the books say.
