> [!definition] Return on Equity
> Return on equity (ROE) is net profit for the owners divided by owners' equity, shown as a percentage.

## Explain

If you put ₹100 into a business and it earns ₹12 a year for you, your ROE is 12%. ROE tells you how well management turns the owners' money into profit.

A consistently high ROE (above about 15% for years) often marks a strong business. But high debt can inflate ROE, so read it with [[debt-to-equity|Debt to Equity Ratio]].

> [!formula] ROE
> $$\text{ROE} = \frac{\text{Net profit for owners}}{\text{Average owners' equity}} \times 100$$
> - Net profit for owners: profit after tax and minority interest
> - Average owners' equity: opening and closing equity averaged

## Example

> [!example] What 9% means
> A ₹1,00,000 investment at book value earns about ₹9,000 a year. A bank FD pays more with no risk.
>
> So Reliance's ROE is below what a deposit pays, yet the market pays 1.75 times book (see [[price-to-book|Price to Book Ratio]]). Investors are betting that new businesses will lift returns.
>
> Different sites define ROE slightly differently (which profit, average vs closing equity), so use one source consistently.

> [!critical] Debt can flatter ROE
> A company can raise ROE just by borrowing more. Always check leverage.
