> [!definition] Inventory Days
> Inventory days is the average number of days a company holds stock before selling it: inventory divided by cost of sales per day.

## Explain

Stock is money sitting on a shelf. The longer it sits, the more cash is locked up and the more risk it becomes outdated or loses value.

Oil refiners hold crude oil and fuel in tanks. Retailers hold goods in stores. So Reliance's number is naturally high.

> [!formula] Inventory days
> $$\text{Inventory days} = \frac{\text{Inventory}}{\text{Cost of sales}} \times 365$$
> - Inventory: stock held at year end
> - Cost of sales: what the goods sold cost

## Data: Reliance

> [!in-practice] Reliance Industries in numbers
> - FY26: 88 days; FY25: 85; FY21: 102
> - FY19: 63 days

*Source: [Screener.in](https://www.screener.in/company/RELIANCE/consolidated/), Reliance Industries, consolidated, viewed 3 Oct 2026. Amounts in ₹ crore unless stated. Used to show how to read the numbers; this is not investment advice.*

## Example

> [!example] Reading 88
> On average Reliance holds about three months' worth of stock before it sells it.
>
> Compare years, not companies: a bakery should be at a few days and a shipbuilder at several hundred.
