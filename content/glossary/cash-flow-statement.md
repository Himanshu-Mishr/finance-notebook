> [!definition] Cash Flow Statement
> The cash flow statement tracks real cash movements in a year, in three parts: from operating activities, from investing activities, and from financing activities.

## Explain

Profit is an accounting idea; cash is real. A company can show a profit and still run out of cash, for example if customers pay late or it spends heavily on new plants.

The three parts answer three questions. Did the business generate cash ([[cash-from-operations|Cash from Operations]])? Where did it invest ([[cash-from-investing|Cash from Investing]])? How was it funded ([[cash-from-financing|Cash from Financing]])?

> [!formula] Net cash flow
> $$\text{Net cash flow} = \text{CFO} + \text{CFI} + \text{CFF}$$
> - CFO: cash from operations
> - CFI: cash from investing
> - CFF: cash from financing

> [!in-practice] Reliance Industries in numbers

> - Investing −₹1,01,089 crore; financing −₹51,549 crore

## Example

> [!example] Adding it up
> 192,113 − 101,089 − 51,549 = 39,475 crore ✓.
>
> Reliance made 1,92,113 crore from its business, spent about 1,01,089 crore on growth, and had net financing outflows of about 51,549 crore (interest, dividends and other payments, net of new borrowing). The rest, 39,475 crore, added to its cash.
