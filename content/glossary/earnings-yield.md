> [!definition] Earnings Yield
> Earnings yield is earnings per share divided by the share price. It is the inverse of the [[pe-ratio|P/E Ratio]].

## Explain

P/E says 'I pay 21 rupees for one rupee of profit'. Earnings yield says 'for every ₹100 I pay, the company earns ₹4.7'. Both say the same thing, but a percentage is easier to compare with a bank FD or a bond yield.

The profit is not paid to you in cash (only the dividend is), but it belongs to you as an owner and builds the company's value.

> [!formula] Earnings yield
> $$\text{Earnings yield} = \frac{\text{EPS}}{\text{Share price}} \times 100$$
> - EPS: see [[eps|Earnings Per Share]]

## Example

> [!example] Reliance vs a bank deposit
> 55.22 ÷ 1,168 = 4.7%.
>
> A bank FD pays interest that is certain. Reliance's 4.7% is an uncertain profit that can grow or shrink. The gap between the two is the extra reward you demand for taking that risk.
