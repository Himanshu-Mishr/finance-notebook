> [!definition] Profit Before Tax
> Profit before tax (PBT) is operating profit plus other income, minus interest and depreciation. Tax is then charged on PBT.

## Explain

PBT is the profit the tax authorities look at. The tax rate shown in results is tax paid as a percentage of PBT. It can differ from the headline rate because of deductions, losses carried forward and different rules for different businesses.

A one-off low or high tax rate can swing net profit, so analysts check the rate year by year.

> [!formula] PBT
> $$\text{PBT} = \text{Operating profit} + \text{Other income} - \text{Interest} - \text{Depreciation}$$
> - Tax rate = tax ÷ PBT

## Data: Reliance

> [!in-practice] Reliance Industries in numbers

## Example

> [!example] From PBT to profit
> 179,065 + 28,846 − 27,061 − 57,688 = 1,23,162.
>
> 123,162 − 95,754 = 27,408 crore of tax and similar items, about 22% of PBT.
>
> If tax had been 17% instead (as in one quarter), net profit would have been about ₹6,000 crore higher. Low tax years inflate profit.
