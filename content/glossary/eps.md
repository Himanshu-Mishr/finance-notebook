> [!definition] Earnings Per Share
> Earnings per share (EPS) is the profit attributable to the company's own shareholders divided by the number of shares.

## Explain

Total profit is hard to compare because companies have different numbers of shares. EPS shrinks profit down to one share so you can compare it with the share price.

If a company issues more shares (for example a bonus issue), EPS falls even if profit does not. Data providers restate older EPS so that years can be compared.

> [!formula] EPS
> $$\text{EPS} = \frac{\text{Profit for shareholders}}{\text{Number of shares}}$$
> - Profit for shareholders: net profit minus the part owned by outside owners of subsidiaries
> - Number of shares: weighted average over the year

## Example

> [!example] Why 95,754 ÷ 1,353 does not give 59.69
> 95,754 ÷ 1,353.2 crore shares = ₹70.8. But reported EPS is ₹59.69.
>
> 59.69 × 1,353.2 = about ₹80,770 crore. That is the profit for Reliance's own shareholders.
>
> The difference, about ₹14,980 crore, is most likely profit that belongs to minority owners of subsidiaries such as Jio Platforms (Reliance owns 66.43%). This is my reading of the numbers. On Screener, click the + beside Net Profit to see the split, or check the annual report.

> [!tip] Use EPS, not total profit, per share
> When comparing with price, always use EPS from the same source and period. Mixing sources causes silly P/E numbers.
