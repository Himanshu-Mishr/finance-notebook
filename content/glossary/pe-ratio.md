> [!definition] P/E Ratio
> The price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio is the share price divided by earnings per share. It says how many years of the current profit you are paying for.

## Explain

If a shop earns ₹10 lakh a year and someone offers ₹2 crore for it, they pay 20 times its yearly profit. A P/E of 20 means the same thing for a listed company.

A high P/E usually means investors expect profit to grow. A low P/E can mean the business is cheap, or that people expect profit to fall. P/E is only useful next to the company's own history and similar companies.

Be clear which earnings are used. 'Trailing' P/E uses the last four quarters (see [[ttm|TTM (Trailing Twelve Months)]]). 'Forward' P/E uses estimated future profit.

> [!formula] P/E
> $$\text{P/E} = \frac{\text{Share price}}{\text{Earnings per share}}$$
> - Share price: today's market price
> - Earnings per share: see [[eps|Earnings Per Share]]

## Data: Reliance

> [!in-practice] Reliance Industries in numbers
> - Price ₹1,168; Screener's Stock P/E 21.2
> - Earnings per share, last four quarters: ₹55.22
> - Earnings per share, FY26 (year to Mar 2026): ₹59.69

*Source: [Screener.in](https://www.screener.in/company/RELIANCE/consolidated/), Reliance Industries, consolidated, viewed 3 Oct 2026. Amounts in ₹ crore unless stated. Used to show how to read the numbers; this is not investment advice.*

## Example

> [!example] Two ways to get a P/E for Reliance
> Last four quarters: 1,168 ÷ 55.22 = 21.15, which matches Screener's 21.2.
>
> FY26 only: 1,168 ÷ 59.69 = 19.6.
>
> Same price, two P/Es. Always check which earnings were used. The inverse, 55.22 ÷ 1,168 = 4.7%, is the earnings yield: profit earned per ₹100 of price.

> [!critical] A low P/E is not automatically cheap
> Profit can be inflated by one-off income (see [[other-income|Other Income]]). A P/E built on a one-off profit looks low but is misleading.
