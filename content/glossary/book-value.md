> [!definition] Book Value
> Book value is total assets minus total liabilities as recorded in the books. Book value per share divides that by the number of shares. The total is also called net worth or shareholders' equity.

## Explain

Think of your own net worth: what you own minus what you owe. Book value is the same idea for a company, using accounting values.

It is a historical record, not a market price. Land bought long ago sits at its old cost, while a brand like Jio or a good reputation may not appear at all.

> [!formula] Book value per share
> $$\text{Book value per share} = \frac{\text{Equity capital} + \text{Reserves}}{\text{Number of shares}}$$
> - Equity capital and reserves: the owners' part of the balance sheet (see [[equity-capital|Equity Capital]] and [[reserves|Reserves]])

## Data: Reliance

> [!in-practice] Reliance Industries in numbers
> - Equity capital ₹13,532 crore + reserves ₹8,90,498 crore = ₹9,04,030 crore (March 2026)
> - Book value ₹668 per share

*Source: [Screener.in](https://www.screener.in/company/RELIANCE/consolidated/), Reliance Industries, consolidated, viewed 3 Oct 2026. Amounts in ₹ crore unless stated. Used to show how to read the numbers; this is not investment advice.*

## Example

> [!example] Checking ₹668
> 9,04,030 crore ÷ 1,353.2 crore shares = ₹668. It matches Screener.
>
> The market price is ₹1,168, so investors value Reliance at about 1.75 times its book value (see [[price-to-book|Price to Book Ratio]]).

> [!note] Where book value works and fails
> It is meaningful for banks and asset-heavy firms. It says little about businesses whose value is brands, software or people.
