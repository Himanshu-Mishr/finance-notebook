> [!definition] Debt to Equity Ratio
> The debt-to-equity ratio is borrowings divided by owners' funds (equity capital plus reserves).

## Explain

It shows how much of the business is financed by lenders compared with owners. A ratio of 1 means one rupee borrowed for each rupee of owners' money.

There is no single right level. Steady businesses (utilities, FMCG) can carry more debt. Cyclical businesses should carry less.

> [!formula] D/E
> $$\text{D/E} = \frac{\text{Borrowings}}{\text{Equity capital} + \text{Reserves}}$$
> - Borrowings: see [[borrowings|Borrowings]]
> - Equity capital and reserves: see [[book-value|Book Value]]

## Example

> [!example] Reliance leverage
> 4,02,962 ÷ 9,04,030 = 0.45.
>
> For every ₹1 of owners' money there is about 45 paise of borrowed money. In March 2023 it was 4,51,664 ÷ 7,15,872 = 0.63.
