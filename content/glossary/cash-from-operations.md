> [!definition] Cash from Operations
> Cash from operations (CFO) is the cash received from customers minus cash paid for running the business, including interest and tax in most presentations.

## Explain

It starts from profit and adjusts for things that are not cash: depreciation is added back, and changes in [[working-capital|Working Capital]] (customers owing money, stock bought, bills unpaid) are accounted for.

Healthy businesses turn most of their operating profit into cash. Screener shows this as 'CFO/OP'.

## Data: Reliance

> [!in-practice] Reliance Industries in numbers
> - FY26 ₹1,92,113 crore; FY25 ₹1,78,703 crore; FY21 only ₹26,958 crore
> - CFO/OP: 113% in FY26 (as shown by Screener), but 37% in FY21

*Source: [Screener.in](https://www.screener.in/company/RELIANCE/consolidated/), Reliance Industries, consolidated, viewed 3 Oct 2026. Amounts in ₹ crore unless stated. Used to show how to read the numbers; this is not investment advice.*

## Example

> [!example] Cash vs profit
> FY26 net profit ₹95,754 crore versus cash from operations ₹1,92,113 crore, twice as much, because ₹57,688 crore of depreciation is not paid in cash that year, and changes in working capital also play a part.
>
> FY21: cash from operations of 26,958 crore was far below operating profit of 80,790, a gap that size usually points to cash tied up in working capital or other timing items.

> [!critical] Profit without cash is a warning
> If profit rises year after year but cash from operations does not, look for sales that are not being collected.
