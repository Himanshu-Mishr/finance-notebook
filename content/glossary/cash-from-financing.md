> [!definition] Cash from Financing
> Cash from financing (CFF) is cash coming in from new loans and share sales, minus cash paid out as repayments, interest and dividends.

## Explain

It shows how the company funded itself. Positive means it raised money. Negative means it paid money back.

A growing company with weak operating cash needs a positive number to survive. A mature company with strong cash often shows a negative number as it pays interest and dividends and repays debt.

## Data: Reliance

> [!in-practice] Reliance Industries in numbers
> - FY26 −₹51,549 crore; FY25 −₹31,891 crore
> - FY19 +₹55,906 crore; FY21 +₹1,01,904 crore

*Source: [Screener.in](https://www.screener.in/company/RELIANCE/consolidated/), Reliance Industries, consolidated, viewed 3 Oct 2026. Amounts in ₹ crore unless stated. Used to show how to read the numbers; this is not investment advice.*

## Example

> [!example] From raising to repaying
> In FY21 Reliance raised about 1,01,904 crore, to fund growth. In FY25 and FY26 the number turned negative (−31,891 and −51,549 crore) as operating cash covered investment and left room for interest, dividends and other payments.
>
> A shift like this shows a company moving from needing outside money to generating its own.
