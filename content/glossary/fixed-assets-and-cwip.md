> [!definition] Fixed Assets and CWIP
> Fixed assets are long-life things a company uses to earn money: plants, buildings, networks, machines. CWIP (capital work in progress) is the cost of assets still under construction.

## Explain

When a new refinery or telecom network is being built, its cost sits in CWIP. When it starts operating, the cost moves to fixed assets and [[depreciation|Depreciation]] begins.

Spending on fixed assets is called capital expenditure, or capex. It shows up as cash going out under investing activities (see [[cash-from-investing|Cash from Investing]]).

## Data: Reliance

> [!in-practice] Reliance Industries in numbers
> - Fixed assets ₹11,24,795 crore (March 2026), up from ₹1,56,458 crore in 2015
> - CWIP ₹2,37,686 crore
> - Investments ₹2,48,332 crore

*Source: [Screener.in](https://www.screener.in/company/RELIANCE/consolidated/), Reliance Industries, consolidated, viewed 3 Oct 2026. Amounts in ₹ crore unless stated. Used to show how to read the numbers; this is not investment advice.*

## Example

> [!example] An asset-heavy giant
> Fixed assets are 52% of total assets (11,24,795 ÷ 21,77,546).
>
> Fixed assets are about 7.2 times what they were in March 2015, a result of building Jio's network, retail stores and new energy projects.
