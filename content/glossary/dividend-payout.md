> [!definition] Dividend Payout Ratio
> The dividend payout ratio is total dividends divided by profit for the year, shown as a percentage.

## Explain

A company can do two things with profit: pay it out, or keep it in the business (the kept part becomes [[reserves|Reserves]]). The payout ratio says how it splits.

A low payout means the company keeps most profit to grow. A high payout suits stable businesses with few new projects.

> [!formula] Payout ratio
> $$\text{Payout ratio} = \frac{\text{Dividends}}{\text{Net profit}} \times 100$$
> - Dividends: total paid to shareholders
> - Net profit: profit for the owners

## Data: Reliance

> [!in-practice] Reliance Industries in numbers
> - Payout about 9% to 12% every year from FY15 to FY26 (10% in FY26)
> - Screener's note: payout has been low at 10.2% of profits over the last 3 years

*Source: [Screener.in](https://www.screener.in/company/RELIANCE/consolidated/), Reliance Industries, consolidated, viewed 3 Oct 2026. Amounts in ₹ crore unless stated. Used to show how to read the numbers; this is not investment advice.*

## Example

> [!example] Where Reliance's profit went in FY26
> Profit for shareholders about ₹80,770 crore. Dividend about 10% of that, roughly ₹8,000 crore.
>
> The remaining about ₹72,000 crore stays in the business and increases reserves.
