> [!definition] Return on Capital Employed
> ROCE is operating profit after depreciation (before interest and tax) divided by capital employed, which is equity plus borrowings.

## Explain

ROE looks at the owners' money alone. ROCE looks at all the money in the business, including loans. It tells you how well the business uses everything it has raised, whatever the source.

Compare ROCE with the interest rate on the company's borrowings. If ROCE is below that rate, each loan rupee costs more than it earns.

> [!formula] ROCE
> $$\text{ROCE} = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Equity} + \text{Borrowings}} \times 100$$
> - EBIT: earnings before interest and tax

## Data: Reliance

> [!in-practice] Reliance Industries in numbers
> - Screener ROCE 10.3%
> - Between 8% and 12% from FY15 to FY26
> - Average cost of borrowing about 6.7%

## Example

> [!example] Earning more than the loan costs
> Capital employed is roughly 9,04,030 + 4,02,962 = 13,06,992 crore.
>
> With ROCE about 10% and borrowing cost about 6.7%, borrowing still adds value, but the margin is thin.
>
> Many sites calculate EBIT slightly differently, so small differences from Screener's 10.3% are normal.
