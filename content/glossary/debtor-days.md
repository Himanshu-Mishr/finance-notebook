> [!definition] Debtor Days
> Debtor days is the average number of days customers take to pay. It is money owed by customers (receivables) divided by sales per day.

## Explain

If you sell on credit, you only get cash when customers pay. Debtor days measures how long that takes. Fewer days means faster collection.

A rising number can mean customers are paying slower, or that the company is pushing sales by giving longer credit.

> [!formula] Debtor days
> $$\text{Debtor days} = \frac{\text{Receivables}}{\text{Sales}} \times 365$$
> - Receivables: money owed by customers

## Data: Reliance

> [!in-practice] Reliance Industries in numbers
> - FY26: 20 days
> - FY15: 5 days; FY19: 19 days

## Example

> [!example] What 20 days means
> Sales of ₹10,55,780 crore are about ₹2,892 crore a day. Twenty days of that is roughly ₹57,850 crore owed by customers at year end.
>
> Debtor days rose from 5 (FY15) to 20 (FY26). A rise like this can come from a change in the customer mix or longer credit terms; the annual report would show which.
