> [!definition] 52-Week High and Low
> The 52-week high and low are the highest and lowest prices a stock reached over the past 52 weeks.

## Explain

They show the range a stock has moved in, and where today's price sits within it. A price near the high says recent buyers have been paying more. A price near the low says sentiment has been poor.

The range is a fact about the past. It does not say where the price goes next.

## Data: Reliance

> [!in-practice] Reliance Industries in numbers
> - High ₹1,612; low ₹1,161; latest close ₹1,168
> - 1-year price change: about −15%

*Source: [Screener.in](https://www.screener.in/company/RELIANCE/consolidated/), Reliance Industries, consolidated, viewed 3 Oct 2026. Amounts in ₹ crore unless stated. Used to show how to read the numbers; this is not investment advice.*

## Example

> [!example] Where Reliance sits in its range
> Below the high: (1,612 − 1,168) ÷ 1,612 = 27.5% down.
>
> Above the low: (1,168 − 1,161) ÷ 1,161 = 0.6% up.
>
> The stock is sitting almost at its one-year low. That alone is not a reason to buy or sell; ask why the price fell (see [[pe-ratio|P/E Ratio]], [[operating-profit-margin|Operating Profit Margin]]).

> [!critical] Do not trade on this number alone
> A stock near its low is not 'due' to rise, and one near its high is not 'due' to fall.
