> [!definition] TTM (Trailing Twelve Months)
> Trailing twelve months (TTM) adds up the latest four quarters of results. It gives the most recent full-year picture without waiting for the financial year to end.

## Explain

Companies report a full year only once a year (March for most Indian companies). In between, TTM keeps the picture current: each new quarter replaces the quarter from a year ago.

P/E on screens usually uses TTM profit so that the ratio is always up to date.

> [!formula] TTM
> $$\text{TTM} = Q_1 + Q_2 + Q_3 + Q_4$$
> - The four most recent quarterly results

## Example

> [!example] Rolling forward
> 254,623 + 264,905 + 294,059 + 309,468 = 11,23,055 ✓.
>
> TTM sales are higher than FY26 because June 2026 (3,09,468) replaced June 2025 (2,43,632).
>
> For profit the move was the other way: TTM net profit is ₹88,167 crore versus ₹95,754 crore in FY26, because the high June 2025 quarter dropped out.
